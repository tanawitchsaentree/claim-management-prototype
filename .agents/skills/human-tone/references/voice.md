# Voice: how a person typing actually sounds

Harvested from real human writing (opinionated practitioner posts, the kind written in one sitting and barely edited). Load this file when the register is direct-punchy, or when the user asks for text that reads like "a person typed this".

These are techniques, and every one of them has a dose. The sample this was harvested from works because the writer believes what he's saying, and the rhythm follows the conviction. Copy the conviction first. The rhythm alone, overdone, becomes its own kind of costume.

## The twelve signatures

1. **Cold open.** The first line is the claim. No context-setting, no "in recent years". A reader should know the writer's position by word ten.

2. **Verdict sentences.** Two to four words that close a thought and stop. "That's gone." "It really isn't." Maximum three per piece; after that they read as a tic.

3. **Speech-rhythm full stops.** Breaking one grammatical sentence at breath points. "The days of polishing five concepts in a design tool. Gone." Grammar purists lose; the reader hears a voice. Direct-punchy register only. Never in formal documents.

4. **One self-question, answered immediately.** "So what do you do? You put something real in front of people." One per piece. Two is a late-night infomercial.

5. **Second person, straight at the reader.** "You're not shipping enough." Not "designers today may find that they...". The writer talks to someone, and the someone is you.

6. **Repetition as a pointer.** "Again, not in a design tool." Re-planting the flag on the one point that matters. Once, maybe twice.

7. **Concession then pivot.** Give the objection its full weight in one sentence, then turn: "Yes, you're being asked to do more in less time. You can fight that. Or you can get faster than it." This is the human version of nuance. Note what it is not: it is not a hedge.

8. **No hedging, anywhere.** The writer owns every claim. If a claim is uncertain, the human move is to say so once, plainly ("I might be wrong about the timeline"), then keep going at full speed.

9. **Contractions, always.** "It isn't" over "it is not", except for deliberate slow emphasis.

10. **Plain nouns and verbs.** The sample contains zero decorative vocabulary across 300+ words. No landscape, no journey, no empower. Things are built, tested, shipped, sold, gone.

11. **Paragraphs of one to three sentences.** One idea each. White space is the punctuation between thoughts.

12. **Real contrast, earned.** "Design was a want, not a need." This passes the strawman test in banlist.md section 2: it names a real prior state and overturns it. This is the ONLY negation contrast pattern worth keeping, and one per piece is the ceiling.

## The one thing never to copy

Real human writing contains genuine slips: a question missing its question mark, a comma splice from typing fast. **Do not fake these.** A model imitating mistakes is doing exactly the thing this whole skill exists to kill: performing humanity instead of writing plainly. The slips are a byproduct of a person typing fast with conviction. Bring the conviction. Skip the typos.

## Calibration pair

BEFORE (clean but generated):
"Designers should consider leveraging AI tools to accelerate their prototyping workflows, as this can significantly reduce iteration time and improve stakeholder alignment."

AFTER (a person typing):
"You should be prototyping in code by now. Not because it's trendy. Because feedback on a working build is a different species of feedback, and AI just made the build cost close to zero. A concept that took two weeks now takes an afternoon. That changes what clients expect from you, whether you like it or not."

Wait. Check that AFTER against the banlist: "Not because it's trendy. Because..." is a negation scaffold. Does it pass the strawman test? Yes: "it's trendy" is a real objection readers actually hold about AI tooling, and the sentence overturns it. One contrast, earned, and it's the only one in the paragraph. This is what the edge of the rule looks like. When in doubt, cut the contrast and state the positive claim.
