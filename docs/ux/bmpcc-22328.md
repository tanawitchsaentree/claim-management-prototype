## Who

Claim handler (main persona). Example: Mara Mustermann, Commercial Claims handler.
- Knows the claim well. Knows which sections (damage types) are settled and which are still moving.
- Juggles 40 to 120 open claims, payments, provider instructions, notes, tasks. Works in bursts between phone calls.
- Closes and reopens sections a few times a week. Reopens a whole claim maybe a few times a month (new invoice, late evidence, litigation). Assumption: frequency from general commercial claims practice, not from a source.
- Changes a section date of loss rarely, usually when one damage happened on a different day than the main event (for example a second storm night, or business interruption that started later).

Claims manager / approver (secondary).
- Looks at Sections to check a handler's work. Should be able to read the Date of loss and Type of coverage columns.
- Assumption: managers and approvers do not close or reopen sections themselves. No source says who may do it; the prototype has no role check (sections.ts has no auth or persona code).

## Story

As a claim handler I want to tick several sections, or all of them, and close or reopen them in one go, so that I do not repeat the same window four times for one claim.

As a claim handler reopening a closed claim I want to pick all or some of its sections in one step, and see what will happen before I commit, so that I do not reopen the wrong ones by accident.

As a claim handler I want each section to show its own date of loss, and change it when one damage happened on a different day, so that reserves, cover checks and reports use the right date.

As a claim handler I want the same words for cover on Entities & damages and on Sections ("Type of coverage"), so that I can match a section to the cover it falls under without translating labels.

Real-world triggers:
- All payments on a claim are done and the handler wants to wrap up every section at once (Confluence 3157631012 item 10).
- A closed claim gets a late invoice or new evidence; the handler reopens the claim and only the sections that are affected (item 11).
- The broker reports that the contents damage happened two days after the building fire (item 13).

## Journey

### A. Close or reopen several sections (AC-01)

1. Open Sections for the claim.
   - Sees: the sections table with a checkbox per section row and one in the header.
   - Knows: which sections are done. Believes: ticking is safe, nothing happens yet.
   - Does: looks over Status and Date of loss.
   - Feels: in control, scanning.
2. Tick sections, or tick the header box to select all.
   - Sees: a grey bar "N sections selected" with Clear selection, "Reopen X sections" and "Close Y sections".
   - Knows: the buttons only count the sections they apply to.
   - Does: unticks the ones they do not want.
   - Feels: reassured by the counts. Risk: confused if the table jumps when the bar appears.
3. Press Close or Reopen.
   - Sees: one section: the usual single window. Two or more: the bulk window that lists the sections by name.
   - Does: picks one reason, confirms.
   - Feels: needs to be sure the list is exactly what they ticked.
4. Done.
   - Sees: a toast naming the sections, the Status chips change, the selection clears. If every section is now closed, the app moves to Claim overview so the claim can be closed.
   - Feels: finished, or surprised if moved without being told why.

### B. Reopen a claim and pick sections (AC-02)

1. On Claim overview of a closed claim, press Reopen Claim.
2. Step 1: pick a reason, optional note, Continue.
3. Step 2: "Select all (4)", a checkbox per closed section, "N of 4 selected". Continue is blocked at zero with a hint.
4. Step 3 (new): what reopens and what stays closed, named. Button says "Reopen claim and N sections". Back keeps the ticks.
5. Done: toast "Claim reopened. N sections reopened. M sections remain closed." with "View sections". Status chip shows Open.

### C. Change a section's date of loss (AC-03)

1. On Sections, read the Date of loss column. A section with its own date shows it, with "Claim: <claim date>" underneath.
2. Section menu (or detail panel Change link), Change date of loss.
3. Window: "Same as the claim (date)" or "A different date for this section" with a date field (no future dates).
4. Continue: confirm window with Field / Original / Updated, same as Edit claim. "Confirm changes" or "Back to editing".
5. Done: toast "Section date of loss updated" with the section name and new date. Column updates.

### D. Read Type of coverage (AC-04, AC-05)

1. FNOL Entities & damages: column and entity panel say "Type of coverage" (was "Covered by").
2. Sections: "Type of coverage" column between Status and Damage, and in the section detail panel.

## Pain points

- Mixed selection. Open and closed sections can be ticked together. If the bar said only "Close" the handler could believe the closed ones would be touched too (AC-01 decision).
- Select all as a blind click. Ticking all and pressing one button can close or reopen more than meant. The ACs answer this with counts on buttons and a named list in the window (AC-01, AC-02 "so select all is never a blind click").
- Being moved off the page. When the last open section closes the app jumps to Claim overview (sections.ts onCloseSection, onCloseSelected). Without a message this feels like a crash or a lost change.
- Two dates, one claim. The handler must see at a glance which sections follow the claim and which have their own date, and what happens when the claim date changes later (AC-03 decision).
- A no-op save. Choosing what is already set and pressing Continue must not look like it saved.
- Back that is really Cancel. A "Back to editing" button that throws the edit away loses the handler's input.
- Closed claim. Nothing on Sections can change while the claim is closed; the handler must learn why and where to go (Reopen Claim on Claim overview). Production only allows section reopen once the claim is open (prod section-overview-table.component.ts:112).
- Cover wording. "Covered by" vs "Type of coverage" on different pages makes matching harder (AC-04).
- Prototype cover data is a default per damage type, not the real match per entity (AC-05 accepted deviation). The column can show a cover that is not the real one.

## Needs

- Before acting: clear row checkboxes, a select all, a live count, and button labels that say what and how many.
- In the window: the exact names of what will change, one reason, a primary button with the count.
- After acting: a toast with names and counts, the row status changed in place, selection cleared, and a reason given if the app moves pages.
- For dates: the effective date per row, a mark when it differs from the claim, the claim date next to it, a confirm with old and new, and a way back to the form that keeps the input.
- For a closed claim: disabled controls that look disabled, plus a short line that says why and where to reopen.
- Same words everywhere: "Type of coverage" on both pages; one way to say "own date vs claim date".

## Checks (owner's rules, applied to every step)

- Visible signal after every action: tick shows the bar; close and reopen give a toast and status change; date change gives a toast and column change; page move must say why.
- No button may read as cancel or go back when it does something else, or the other way round ("Back to editing" must go back to editing).
- Confirm before commit, toast after: the reason window or confirm step before, toast after, for every change.
- Stay in context: no layout jumps, no surprise page moves.
- Same thing looks the same everywhere: same bar as Claims list, same confirm as Edit claim, same wording for own vs claim date, same title style for windows.
- Plain language: names not IDs, no internal codes.
- Only existing NDBX / app components: app-bulk-selection-bar, confirm-dialog, bulk-closure-modal, nx-checkbox, nx-context-menu, toast-stack.
